## Brainstorm fragments

**Grounding**
- **Feet-on-floor scan (2 min):** Name three physical sensations before touching your phone. Purpose: return to your body, not your notifications.
- **Sunlight + water (5 min):** Take light and a glass of water. Purpose: anchor circadian rhythm and basic biology.
- **Box breathing (5 min):** Inhale/hold/exhale/hold, 4 counts each. Purpose: lower reactivity and create a calm gap.
- **Gratitude naming (3 min):** Say one specific thing you're glad exists. Purpose: set emotional baseline.
- **Unsent journal (5 min):** Dump whatever is circling in your head. Purpose: empty the mental vault so thinking can start.

**Productivity**
- **One-line plan (3 min):** Decide the single outcome that makes today a win. Purpose: give the day a protagonist.
- **Deep work block (60–90 min):** First meaningful task in 25-min sprints with breaks. Purpose: protect high-value work before the world wakes.
- **Movement (20 min):** Bodyweight routine or fast walk. Purpose: wake the body, boost cognition.
- **Reading/input (15 min):** One chapter, article, or poem. Purpose: feed the mind before it must output.
- **Inbox/triage (10 min):** Scan calendar and messages, time-box each. Purpose: reduce surprise, make a clean transition.

## Possible orderings

**A. The Protagonist Order**
Wake → feet scan → water/sunlight → breathing → movement → one-line plan → deep work block → buffer (shower/coffee/set desk) → start work.
Flow: body first, then direction, then attack. Buffer keeps 9:00 start safe.

**B. The Writer’s Dawn**
Wake → unsent journal → gratitude → reading → one-line plan → deep work block → buffer → start work.
Flow: page before people, direction after introspection. Best for thinkers; no exercise in this order.

**C. The Grounded Operator**
Wake → feet scan → water/sunlight → movement → shower → inbox/calendar triage → one-line plan → deep work block → buffer → start work.
Flow: calm body, then order the outside world, then focus. Most realistic for busy mornings.

**Story to retell:** “I win the first hour before the world asks anything of me.” People retell this because it gives them a single line to defend the morning — and a reason to leave the phone untouched.
