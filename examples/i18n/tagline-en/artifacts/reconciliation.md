## Dialectical sublation of the four drafts

The four drafts are not four options. They are four projections of the same object—Maru, a circle—onto four planes: the market, the heart, the language, and time. Each projection is true. But a projection is not the thing itself. A circle cannot be walked on if it is only projected.

---

### 1. Negation

**Strategist — negated as totality.**  
The strategist is right that a tagline must *work*: it must position the brand, cut through category noise, and be remembered long enough to change behavior. “Clean ocean. Clean streets. Clean kicks.” is rhythmically and strategically alive.  
But the strategist’s one-sidedness is that the wearer becomes a *target*, the sea becomes a *value proposition*, and sustainability becomes a *feature*. The loop is closed on paper, but the wearer is left outside it. The tagline becomes an arrow aimed at a demographic, not a circle held open for a person to enter.

**Humanist — negated as totality.**  
The humanist is right that a tagline must *mean* something to the person who wears the shoe. “Your footprint shouldn’t leave one” is ethically direct. It turns an ordinary action—walking—into a relation of care.  
But the humanist’s one-sidedness is that the wearer is reduced to a moral agent. The urban, aesthetic, performative desire for the shoe disappears. The city, the style, the pleasure of movement—all are sacrificed to a pure intention. The tagline becomes a sermon, and a sermon does not sell sneakers.

**Differentiator — negated as totality.**  
The differentiator is right that Maru must be *unmistakable*. “One circle. One ocean. Every step closes the loop.” makes the brand’s name itself the message. It is singular, rhythmic, and ownable.  
But the differentiator’s one-sidedness is that uniqueness can become a sealed room. “Your sole has a soul” is a good pun, but a pun ends in itself. It does not move the wearer from the street back to the sea. Difference without direction is noise.

**Storyteller — negated as totality.**  
The storyteller is right that the shoe has a biography. “Made from the sea. Made for the city.” gives the material a past and a future. The wearer steps into an ongoing narrative.  
But the storyteller’s one-sidedness is linearity. “From coast to city” is a one-way journey. The story ends when the product is sold. The circle is not yet closed, because the wearer’s own step has not been asked to continue the return.

Each draft is true, and each is false insofar as it claims to be the whole truth. Strategy without soul is a shell; soul without strategy is a sermon; uniqueness without story is a riddle; story without step is a legend.

---

### 2. Preservation

What must be preserved is not the drafts’ wording, but the insight buried in each.

**Preserve the strategist’s demand for closure.**  
A tagline must be strategically effective. It must win a place in the market against rivals who offer performance without environment. Without this, the sea’s protection is only an intention, not a brand. The final tagline must be *ownable* and *scalable*.

**Preserve the humanist’s demand for agency.**  
The wearer must not be a passive consumer of a message. The tagline must make the wearer feel that their ordinary daily step is an act of participation. Without this, sustainability is a label, not a life.

**Preserve the differentiator’s demand for identity.**  
“Maru” is a circle. The tagline must exploit that geometry—not as decoration, but as the very signature of the shoe. The tagline must be impossible to imagine on another sneaker.

**Preserve the storyteller’s demand for time.**  
The tagline must make the material’s origin visible: the coast, the plastic, the sea. It must also give the wearer a future. Without this, the tagline is a static claim rather than a living movement.

What is common to all four preserved moments? **The circle.**  
Maru is a circle. Circular economy is a circle. A zero footprint is a zero. A step forward that is also a return is a circle. The drafts were all circling around the same thing; none of them was able to say it as a whole.

---

### 3. Elevation

The higher dimension is **performative circularity**: a tagline that does not *describe* a circle, but *enacts* one.

In this elevated framework, Maru is not an object made from the sea and used in the city. Maru is the middle term through which the sea and the city become one continuous movement. The sea gives material; the city gives steps; the shoe turns one into the other; and every step, because it is a step *on* a material that came from the sea, is a return.

The old environmental message is “leave no trace.” Maru’s message must be stronger: **leave a trace that returns.** The wearer does not need to step lightly in the sense of disappearing. The wearer can run hard, walk far, wear the shoe through the city—and every footprint is a stroke in a circular tide.

This is why the rival framework of “high performance vs. environmental consideration” is also sublated. A rival shoe performs linearly: speed from point A to point B. Maru performs circularly: every stride returns to its origin. The wearer no longer has to choose between running well and living well. Running well *is* living well, if each step is a return.

The final tagline must therefore have the grammar of recurrence: a step that returns, a wearing that gives back, a circle that closes. It must not command the wearer to “save the sea,” because that leaves the sea outside. It must not merely narrate “from tide to tread,” because that leaves the story behind. It must make the wearer the pivot of the cycle.

A tagline shaped by this sublation would not be selected from the drafts. It would be generated from them. It would sound like:

**“Maru: Every step returns to the sea.”**

Here the step is the strategist’s urban target in motion; the return is the humanist’s ethical agency; “Maru” is the differentiator’s circle; and “to the sea” is the storyteller’s origin and destination. The tagline is not a statement *about* a circle. It is a circle spoken in one breath.

---

### 4. Transcendental viewpoint

From the height of the sublation, the original conflict was never a conflict.

A circle has no beginning and no end. It has no inside and no outside. The sea and the city are not opposites; the coastline is the line where the sea becomes walkable. The urban dweller’s step is a small tide. Strategy, compassion, uniqueness, and story are not four competing claims; they are four points on the same circumference.

The conflict only appeared because language forced a circular reality into a straight line. The final tagline does not “resolve” the conflict by choosing a middle position. It makes the conflict unrecognizable by completing the loop. “Every step returns to the sea” does not say that the shoe is good for the environment. It says that the wearer’s own movement is the return. The shoe is not a product with a message. The shoe is the place where the sea’s material becomes the sea’s next wave—one step at a time.
