# The Morning of Mercy: Where You Actually Are

| Time | Activity | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | **The Cut (mindfulness).** Wake; a glass of water; no phone, no words. Sit with whatever is present—including the emptiness or dread you'd rather skip. | The first act is honesty, not quarantine: you learn what you're carrying before deciding to carry it. |
| 0:10–0:20 | **The Body (light + movement).** Open the curtains to daylight; move gently or briskly as the body asks—and obey its request at least once. | You consent to being finite flesh; grounding begins in the knees, not the mind. |
| 0:20–0:35 | **Care (wash, dress, food).** Shower, dress, breakfast, all screenless; if another human is near, give them one full minute of your eyes. | Nourishment is a ritual of worthiness—and the first face you meet should sometimes be someone else's. |
| 0:35–0:50 | **The Decision (planning).** Write the task you're avoiding, raw; then one sentence: "If all else fails, this is the win"—but the win may be a kindness, a repair, or mere presence, not a task. | A covenant with the day, but covenants can be made with grace, not only with productivity. |
| 0:50–1:05 | **The Open (learning).** Check messages, read something nourishing, or simply walk into the day. | The self is now constituted; the world may enter. |
| Always | **The Mercy.** If the structure collapses—a crying child, despair, sheer refusal—do not fail the routine: sit one minute with no agenda and begin already broken. | The routine's true proof is that you begin from where you are, not from where you should be. |

**Overall intent:** To start the day not with clarity and calm, but with mercy—for the self you find in the mirror and the world that will inevitably break that calm.
